CWM is ServiceNow's work-management product. It just shipped a dashboard that measures the sprint. This case study proposes what comes next: AI that explains the sprint, and a board that knows which view the work needs.

The problem

The dashboard says the sprint is slipping, not why. And CWM offers List, Kanban and Gantt without saying which one the moment needs.

The proposal

Daily Pulse explains and forecasts the sprint. Stage-aware views pick the right view and set it up.

Why CWM

It sits on the same database as the incidents, approvals and plans that actually cause the slip.

My perspective: 2.5 years as a software engineer on CWM across four SPM teams (sprint metrics, data grids, agile components), plus 50+ customer case tasks in PPM and Project Workspace. I watched the product grow, and I watched people not start their day in it.

Act 02 — The two gaps

Two gaps between opening CWM and knowing what to do.

One day in Priya's life as a scrum master shows both.

9:40
Twenty minutes to standup
  • The dashboard shows the burndown above the ideal line.
  • She opens EAP, ITSM, PPM and email to find out why.
  • Fifteen minutes later she has the cause, and acts on it in three more places.
→ Gap 01
14:00
Planning the next sprint
  • The board opens in Kanban, the view it was left in.
  • Planning needs estimates and owners side by side. That's List.
  • Then the release date against a dependency. That's Gantt. Each switch, she resets columns and grouping.
→ Gap 02
Gap 01 · Judgment

The dashboard says what. Never why.

User sees
The line is above ideal.
User needs
Which items caused it, why, and what to change before standup.
Why it's hard
The cause sits in other modules and in free text: an incident, a pending approval, a work note saying "waiting on vendor".
Cost
~15 minutes every morning across four modules, rebuilding cause and effect by hand.
Evidence
Refocusing after an app switch takes ~9.5 minutes (Cornell / Qatalog). Here, every switch is inside one platform.
Gap 02 · Guidance

Three views. No help choosing.

User sees
List, Kanban and Gantt, all given equal weight, plus a side panel of columns, lanes and grouping.
User needs
The view that fits the stage of work they're in, already set up for it.
Why it's hard
The board knows its data, not what the team is doing with it today. So the choice falls to the user, every time the stage changes.
Cost
The wrong view hides what the stage needs: date conflicts don't show in Kanban, flow doesn't show in List.
Evidence
Configuration divergence was the most common root cause in escalations I triaged. ClickUp reviews name "which view do I use for what" as top early friction.
The bar for both

A team of six gets something useful from a board in ten minutes, without opening the settings panel.

Act 03 — Why this needs AI

If a query can do it, it isn't the AI part.

Every capability below starts where a report or a rule stops.

The question
A query or rule gets you
Needs AI because
Why did the line move?
Which items are late
The reason is written, not stored. "Vendor creds expired" lives in a work note, not a field.
What's blocking us from outside?
Records someone linked
Most blockers are never linked. Matching an incident to a story means reading both and checking they touch the same service.
Will we make it?
Average velocity
A forecast learned from this team's own history: cycle time by item type, item age, how far estimates usually miss.
What should we cut?
A list of unstarted items
Weighing dependencies, the plan milestone and the sprint goal, then explaining two or three trade-offs in plain words.
Which view does this moment need?
A view menu
Reading the stage of work from what the team is doing. Rules seed it on day one; it learns each team after that.

The view arrives configured: Kanban lanes that match how the team really moves work, List with points and owner columns, Gantt with dependencies on.

"Your team plans in Gantt, not List: 4 sprints running. Make that the team default?"
AILearns which view each stage really uses on this team. The lead approves; new members start with it.
Act 06 — Where it lives

One place to start your day. One place to check a team.

Today CWM has a personal work dashboard and a board dashboard side by side, and users pick between them. The direction: the Pulse replaces the personal dashboard. It doesn't sit next to it.

My Work

Daily Pulse

  • Your day, across every board and module.
  • Replaces the personal dashboard.
  • The first screen when you open CWM.
Inside each board

Board dashboard

  • The shipped sprint dashboard, unchanged.
  • One team's health: burndown, scope, status.
  • Opens on its own in the Review stage.

The Pulse never redraws a chart; it links to the board's dashboard. Two surfaces, two jobs, nothing to choose between.

Act 07 — Why CWM

The platform owns the reasoning.
CWM owns the morning.

The hardest objection is internal: cross-module AI should be a platform feature every user gets. Half of that is right.

Conceded

Build the intelligence as Now Assist platform skills, reusable anywhere. No private AI stack in CWM. The open question is where a person receives it and acts on it.

A team decides on a board

A notification reaches one person, then sends them to five apps. On a board the team sees, discusses and acts on the same item.

The joins already exist

Project Workspace, EAP and Connected Work already meet in CWM. A new surface would rebuild all three.

An accuracy bar you can hit

One audience and one set of joins first. Expand once the predictions hold.

Act 10 — Research

What this rests on, and what would break it.

What I used
  • 2.5 years building CWM, including sprint metrics and agile components.
  • 50+ customer case tasks in PPM and Project Workspace, in customers' own words.
  • Escalation triage: configuration divergence was the most common root cause.
  • An adjacent AI feature that lost adoption sharply after launch. Why trust is a kill signal here, not polish.
  • Desk research: ClickUp, Jira, Linear, Asana, Monday.com on views and setup cost.
What I haven't done yet
  • Who: 8–10 scrum masters and delivery leads at three CWM customers, plus three teams that left for another tool. Two weeks, no engineering time.
  • Gap 01: "After seeing the burndown off track, what did you do in the next ten minutes, and where did you find the reason?"
  • Gap 02: "Which view do you plan in, and which do you run standup in? When did you last change the columns?"
  • Would falsify it: leads say the dashboard is enough, or they already use one view per stage without thinking. Then the money goes to EAP integration, and only "Set up for the job" ships.
Act 11 — The hard calls

Four objections, answered short.

01
"Isn't this the new dashboard with AI on top?"
The tensionA briefing arriving months after the dashboard can read as a wrapper, or a second place answering the same question.
Where I landThe dashboard is the input, not the rival. It measures; the Pulse explains and forecasts from the dashboard's own snapshots, and links to it rather than redrawing it.
02
"Permissions decide what a user can see. Zero setup can't bypass that."
The tensionShowing an ITSM incident to a user without an ITSM role either leaks data or shows nothing useful.
Where I landTwo tiers. Everyone sees the impact ("blocked by an open incident, sprint ends Thursday") with no record data. The record and its link show only to roles that already have access. First thing to validate with a platform architect.
03
"Picking the view for me takes away control."
The tensionA workspace that rearranges itself is disorienting, and stage detection will sometimes guess wrong.
Where I landSuggest, never lock. One click to change, and the choice is remembered. Today's default is just the last view someone left open, which is a worse guess. A wrong view costs a click; a wrong risk alert costs trust.
04
"What happens when the AI is wrong?"
The tensionOne confident wrong claim before standup and the Pulse gets ignored for good.
Where I landShow the source, let users mark it wrong. Every claim links to the note or record it came from. If accuracy falls below 70%, the Pulse drops back to the chart and says so.
